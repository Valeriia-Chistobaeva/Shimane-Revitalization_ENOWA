# APP Name

**ENOWA** えのわ / 縁の輪​

Enowa is the proposed name for a new way to connect people, opportunities and communities across Shimane Prefecture. Motherhood and family support are central themes within this wider vision.

# APP Idea

Enowa is a proposed bilingual Japanese–English application that brings information, local opportunities and human support together in one accessible place. It is intended for use across the whole of Shimane Prefecture, including smaller and rural municipalities.

The app helps people prepare for pregnancy and parenthood, find support during and after childbirth, build connections in their communities, and pursue financial independence and sustainable careers. It is especially attentive to women considering motherhood without a partner, single parents and people without a nearby support network, while remaining useful to families and the wider community.

Shimane already has municipal services, healthcare providers, employment support and community organisations. Enowa would help people navigate and connect with these existing resources; it would complement them rather than assume they do not exist. The application is one part of a broader initiative that also envisages a one-stop family support centre, a structured preparation programme for women considering motherhood without a partner, and a connection between motherhood and long-term career development. These are proposed components, not established services.

**How it would work:** A user chooses a municipality and the topics relevant to them, explores local guidance and services, and can request contact with a real support person. With the user's consent, a coordinator can help identify next steps and connect them to appropriate organisations. Support should begin before a crisis and continue as needs change.

# Key Objective

**Make it easier for people in Shimane to find timely, continuous and practical support for family life, community participation and financial independence.**

- Help users understand and access relevant services before, during and after pregnancy and childbirth.
- Reduce isolation by connecting people with local communities and real support providers.
- Help parents plan for childcare, housing, finances and stable, adequately paid work compatible with family responsibilities.
- Support informed, voluntary decisions about motherhood, including for women without a partner; the programme should neither encourage nor discourage a particular family choice.
- Contribute to Shimane's wider revitalisation by making it easier for people to build sustainable lives and careers in the prefecture. Enowa is one proposed contribution, not a standalone solution to population decline.

The central problem is coordination and accessibility: the existence of individual services does not automatically mean that everyone knows about them or can navigate them easily. Local needs and gaps should be validated with residents and service providers before implementation.

# Target Audience

- **Women considering pregnancy or motherhood**, including those considering motherhood without a partner, who want to understand their options and prepare practically.
- **Pregnant women and new mothers** seeking medical, emotional, financial and practical support through pregnancy, birth and postpartum recovery.
- **Single mothers and other single parents** looking for childcare, financial assistance, housing, career development and community connections.
- **Parents and families across Shimane** who need reliable local information, childcare or opportunities to connect with others.
- **People with limited local support networks**, including newcomers and residents of rural communities who may find services harder to discover or reach.
- **Support providers and partner organisations**, including municipalities, health professionals, employment services, community groups and employers, who could help keep information accurate and provide referrals.

Japanese and English are intended as the app's initial languages. Content and referral arrangements should reflect each municipality's actual services and eligibility rules.

# Key Features

## 1. Local support directory

A searchable, municipality-specific directory brings together public services, hospitals, midwives, childcare, welfare organisations, community groups and employment support. Entries should explain what each service offers, how to contact it, opening hours, eligibility where applicable, and when its details were last checked. Users can filter by location, topic and language.

**Implementation note:** Work with municipalities and providers to verify listings and assign responsibility for keeping them current. A listing or referral should not imply that a service is available, free or accepting new users without confirmation.

## 2. Connect with a real person

Users can request a conversation with a support coordinator or be directed to an appropriate professional or organisation. The coordinator can help them understand options, prepare for appointments and navigate referrals across health, childcare, financial and employment services.

**Implementation note:** Define staffing, response times, languages and referral procedures with participating organisations. Show users what happens after a request and obtain consent before sharing their information. The app should clearly identify urgent and emergency contacts rather than promise immediate assistance.

## 3. Personalised support journey

A simple optional profile or checklist helps users organise tasks and resources around their circumstances: considering motherhood, pregnancy, birth, postpartum recovery, childcare or returning to work. Relevant reminders and suggested next steps can help users prepare early and follow through.

**Implementation note:** Let users browse core information without disclosing sensitive details. Recommendations are prompts for discussion, not eligibility decisions or medical advice; users remain in control of their choices and notifications.

## 4. Pregnancy, childbirth and postpartum guidance

Offer clear explanations and links to local prenatal care, childbirth preparation, midwives, postpartum services, breastfeeding support and mental health resources. Include information on appropriate prenatal and postpartum physical health and recovery activities, with guidance to consult a qualified professional about individual needs.

**Implementation note:** Have qualified professionals review health content, link to authoritative sources and provide review dates. The app should connect users to medical care, not diagnose conditions or replace a clinician.

## 5. Preparation for motherhood without a partner

Provide a structured, optional preparation pathway covering practical choices, costs, benefits, housing, employment, childcare, legal information, emergencies and building a support network. Include routes to personalised medical, psychological, financial and legal consultations.

**Implementation note:** Present options without judgement and refer users to qualified advisers for individual circumstances. The pathway is for informed preparation, not a requirement for receiving help.

## 6. Financial planning, benefits and housing

Bring together budgeting guidance, anticipated family expenses, maternity-related assistance, single-parent benefits, training support and housing resources. Explain where to apply and what information may be required, with links to the responsible authorities.

**Implementation note:** Make clear whether assistance is a benefit, subsidy or loan, and direct users to the relevant municipality for current amounts and eligibility. Any budget estimate should state its assumptions and avoid presenting an estimate as an entitlement.

## 7. Childcare and practical help

Help users discover childcare options, local family services, home assistance and support for everyday needs during pregnancy and after birth. Explain how to enquire about availability, eligibility and application procedures.

**Implementation note:** Coordinate information with local providers; actual places, fees and operating hours must be confirmed with them. Consider access barriers such as distance and transport in rural areas.

## 8. Careers and long-term independence

Connect users with career counselling, job-search services, training, qualifications and local employers. Highlight stable and adequately paid roles and, where available, flexible, remote or hybrid opportunities compatible with childcare. Help users consider childcare and training arrangements alongside a career plan.

**Implementation note:** Collaborate with existing employment services and employers. Show relevant job conditions clearly and avoid implying that a listing guarantees a position or a particular working arrangement.

## 9. Community and local opportunities

Make it easier to find mother-and-child groups, parent clubs, local events, peer support and other community activities across Shimane. This extends Enowa's purpose beyond a directory of formal services and helps people build connections before they need urgent help.

**Implementation note:** Provide clear information about organisers, locations and accessibility. If direct community interaction is introduced, establish moderation, reporting and privacy arrangements first.

## 10. Accessible, trustworthy and private experience

Provide Japanese and English navigation, plain-language explanations and accessible design. Protect personal information, collect only what is needed, and give users clear choices about sharing and deleting their data.

**Implementation note:** Agree on content ownership, translation review, consent, security and retention with implementation partners. Ensure alternative ways to access help for people who cannot or prefer not to use the app.

## 11. Learning whether the initiative works

With appropriate privacy protections, evaluate whether people can find and reach support and whether referrals lead to useful help. Potential measures include awareness of services, completed contacts or referrals, time to a response, user satisfaction, access to postpartum support, career counselling and longer-term employment outcomes.

**Implementation note:** Establish definitions and baseline measures with partners and users before setting targets. Treat demographic change as a long-term contextual outcome, not proof that the app alone caused a change.
